Houston's receiving corps looked settled two weeks ago. It does not anymore. **Jayden Higgins** tore his ACL in practice and is done for the season, and that single injury is already rerouting targets across the Texans' offense.

## The hole Higgins leaves behind

Higgins was ticketed to start opposite **Nico Collins**, the clear WR1 in Houston's passing game. Losing a starting outside receiver before Week 1 even finished does not just cost the Texans a body, it costs them a route combination. Every target Higgins would have run now has to land somewhere else on the depth chart.

## Nico Collins does not get easier, he gets more attention

The instinct is to assume Collins simply inherits every look Higgins would have drawn. It rarely works that cleanly. Defensive coordinators know Houston has one proven outside threat left, and they will shade extra attention his way on early downs. Collins remains the safest bet in this offense, but the raw target bump is not guaranteed to be as large as the box score projections suggest.

## Dalton Schultz is the quiet winner

The player actually positioned to benefit is **Dalton Schultz**. He was already a high volume target in this offense last season, finishing with 82 receptions for 777 yards, tied for third among tight ends in catches. With an outside receiver spot now empty, Houston's short and intermediate passing game funnels even more directly through its tight end. Schultz was never going to be a league winning name, but a tight end who was already seeing that much volume, working behind a receiver corps that just got thinner, plays like a real weekly starter in most standard formats now, not a streaming option.

## What to do with this

Do not bench Collins, and do not chase a name to directly replace Higgins, since no single Texans receiver profiles as a clean one for one swap. Instead, look at your own tight end spot. If Schultz is sitting on your bench or on the waiver wire, this is the week the role change is real and not just a hot take.
