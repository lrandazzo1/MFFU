RecapSeptember 14, 2026

Week 1 delivered a scorigami, a rushing record, and two nail biters

By FSN Desk

The 2026 season opened with one of the wilder Sundays in recent memory. A final score that had never happened before, a future Hall of Famer climbing the all time record book, and two games that came down to the final possession. Here is what actually happened in Week 1, matchup by matchup, and what it means heading into Week 2.

Chicago sets a record nobody saw coming

The Bears beat the Panthers 59-37, and the final score itself made history: it was the first time that exact scoreline had ever occurred in an NFL game, a so called "scorigami." The two teams combined for 96 points, a new record for a season opener. Caleb Williams accounted for four total touchdowns, two through the air and two on the ground, while D'Andre Swift did the heavy lifting on the ground with 124 rushing yards and three touchdowns of his own.

Derrick Henry keeps making history

Baltimore rolled past Indianapolis 41-23, and Derrick Henry was the story again. He rushed for 144 yards and three touchdowns, and the third score pushed his career rushing touchdown total past Marcus Allen for third all time in NFL history. Henry has now scored in a way few backs at his age are supposed to, and Week 1 was a reminder that the workload is not slowing down.

Detroit needed overtime, and Jahmyr Gibbs needed every carry

The Lions escaped New Orleans with a 31-30 overtime win, and Jahmyr Gibbs carried the offense to get there: 29 carries for 156 yards and two touchdowns on the ground, plus five catches for 30 yards through the air, 186 total yards in all. The one blemish was a lost fumble, but on a day this physical it did not come close to costing Detroit the game.

Buffalo does it again in the fourth quarter

Josh Allen turned in one of the cleaner stat lines of the week against Houston: 20 of 29 passing for 334 yards and two touchdowns with no interceptions, and six carries for 23 yards and two more scores on the ground. The Bills needed a late, record setting game winning drive to close it out, which has become something of a habit for this offense in September.

Philadelphia survives Washington

The Eagles held on for a 24-22 win over the Commanders, and it was closer than the final margin suggests. Philadelphia had to stop a two point conversion attempt in the final minutes to keep Washington from getting a look at overtime.

What it means for your lineup

One week is one week. A blowout scorigami, a record chase, and two one score finishes make for a great Sunday, but they are not a full sample size. Treat Week 1 as a data point, not a verdict: workhorse backs who carried the ball 25-plus times, like Gibbs and Swift, are worth trusting again next week, and a quarterback who protected the ball while still scoring through the air and on the ground, like Allen, is exactly the profile that holds up over a season. Do not bench anyone off one bad script, and do not chase anyone off one great one either.
